# Week 9 · Situate and Explain Your AI Design

Prepare for the next class on **Wednesday 4 November 2026**.

## Assignment

Situate your AI design in relevant scholarship and make it explainable: show what you designed, explain the reasoning behind its choices and their historical and/or cultural implications, and discuss how your work contributes to a scholarly conversation. Be prepared to explain your technical choices as well.

Present your work in **ten minutes**, showing its design one part at a time: for example, plot, graphics, game mechanics, characters, environment, or the parts relevant to your own project. Use **Reslides**, our React slide tool, as the medium for your presentation; learning to use it is also part of the exercise. You can embed a webpage you have made. Reslides access and setup details will be supplied separately.

For each part, explain:

1. **What is your design?** Show the part and what it lets someone do or see.
2. **What are its historical and/or cultural implications?** Explain the specific argument or significance, including what makes a game playable where relevant.
3. **Who has discussed those implications?** Engage with **three relevant scholarly works in total**, explaining what they argue and how they relate to your design.
4. **What does your design contribute?** Show how its choices or mechanisms respond to, extend, or question that conversation.

Explain your technical stack: why you chose its technologies, what they can do, and their limits.

## Research and records

Use the course's [design document and research journal structures](../examples/research-documents/README.md). Follow these steps, returning to earlier ones as your ideas develop:

1. **Think deeply and discuss.** What is interesting about your project, and why? Record your ideas in your design document and research journal. Ask AI for ideas, then respond, question and develop them through discussion.
2. **Search.** From the workspace root, ask OpenCode to use the [scholarly-search skill](../../.opencode/skills/scholarly-search/SKILL.md) to search OpenAlex. Search Google Scholar, Bing, DuckDuckGo or Baidu in your browser with the agent's help. For browser automation, ask OpenCode to set up and use **agent-browser**; the skill explains the setup and permitted routes. Try Deep Research if available. **Do not use OpenCode's simple web search for this task.** Record your actual queries and useful leads.
3. **Organise and assign reading.** Make a Markdown bibliography of relevant works. Ask agents to read accessible works for material related to your ideas and return the source and page or other precise locator for each finding. An unread search result is a lead, not a finding.
4. **Read and think for yourself.** Go back to the key passages, check their context and record your own thoughts in the research journal. Distinguish an author's argument, your interpretation and an agent's suggestion.
5. **Integrate into your design.** Ask an agent to help integrate your thinking into the relevant parts of the design document; review the changes yourself. Add the thinking and reasons to your research journal. Integrating changes the design where appropriate; it is more than appending a note.
6. **Synthesise and loop.** Develop supported points connecting your design, its historical and/or cultural significance, the scholars' arguments and your contribution to their conversation. Search, read and revise again as needed. Every piece of evidence must have a source and a page or other precise locator where available.
7. **Make an outline.** When you have enough supported points, organise them into an outline for your ten-minute Reslides presentation. Show each relevant part of the design and engage with three scholarly works in total.

Use the same curiosity to learn your **technical stack**: ask agents to help you understand each technology and how the parts connect, then explain your choices, capabilities and limits in your own words. Keep your bibliography in Markdown; no separate reference manager is needed.

# 第九週 · 將你的 AI 設計放進學術討論，並清楚解釋它

請為 **2026 年 11 月 4 日（星期三）**的下次課堂做好準備。

## 本週作業

將你的 AI 設計放進相關的學術討論，並讓它能夠被清楚理解：展示你設計了甚麼，解釋各項選擇背後的思考及其歷史和／或文化意義，並討論你的作品如何對學術對話作出貢獻。你也需要能夠解釋自己的技術選擇。

請準備一個**十分鐘展示**，逐一展示設計的各個部分，例如情節、畫面、遊戲機制、角色、環境，或其他與你的專案相關的部分。展示使用我們的 React 投影片工具 **Reslides**；學習使用這個工具也是本次練習的一部分。你也可以嵌入自己製作的網頁。Reslides 的使用與設定方式會另行提供。

對每一個部分，請說明：

1. **你的設計是甚麼？** 展示這個部分，以及它讓使用者能夠做甚麼或看到甚麼。
2. **它有甚麼歷史和／或文化意義？** 解釋具體的論點或意義；如果是遊戲，也請說明它如何讓人玩、哪裏有趣。
3. **哪些學者討論過這些問題？** 整個展示需要結合**三項相關學術文獻**，說明它們各自的論點，以及它們與你的設計有甚麼關係。
4. **你的設計作出了甚麼貢獻？** 說明設計選擇或機制如何回應、延伸或質疑這些學術討論。

同時解釋你的技術架構（technical stack）：為甚麼選擇這些技術、它們能做到甚麼，以及它們的限制。

## 研究與紀錄

請參考課程提供的[設計文件與研究日誌結構](../examples/research-documents/README.md)，按照以下步驟進行；隨着想法發展，可以隨時回到前面的步驟。

1. **深入思考與討論。** 你的專案有甚麼令你感興趣？為甚麼？把自己的想法記在設計文件和研究日誌中。請 AI 提出想法，再回應、追問，透過來回討論發展自己的思路。
2. **搜尋。** 從工作區根目錄啟動 OpenCode，請它使用[學術搜尋技能](../../.opencode/skills/scholarly-search/SKILL.md)搜尋 OpenAlex；也可以在 agent 的協助下，自己在瀏覽器中搜尋 Google Scholar、Bing、DuckDuckGo 或百度。若要使用瀏覽器自動化，請 OpenCode 設定並使用 **agent-browser**；技能文件說明了設定方法及可使用的途徑。如果你有 Deep Research，也可以嘗試。**本次作業不要使用 OpenCode 的簡單網頁搜尋功能。** 記下實際使用的搜尋詞和有用的文獻線索。
3. **整理書目並分配閱讀。** 用 Markdown 整理相關文獻，請 agents 閱讀能取得的作品，尋找與你的想法相關的內容，並為每一項發現附上來源和頁碼或其他精確位置。未讀過的搜尋結果只是線索。
4. **親自閱讀與思考。** 回到關鍵段落，核查上下文，並把自己的想法記入研究日誌。區分作者的論點、你自己的理解和 agent 的建議。
5. **整合到設計中。** 請 agent 協助把你的思考整合到設計文件的相關部分，並由你自己檢查修改；同時把想法與修改原因加入研究日誌。整合是按需要改進設計，不只是把一段文字附加在文件末尾。
6. **綜合並反覆推進。** 將設計、它的歷史和／或文化意義、學者的論點，以及你的設計對學術討論的貢獻連起來，形成有依據的論點。按需要再次搜尋、閱讀和修改。每一條證據都必須註明來源，並在可以取得時附上頁碼或其他精確位置。
7. **整理大綱。** 當你積累了足夠有依據的論點，將它們整理成十分鐘 Reslides 展示的大綱。展示設計的各個相關部分，並結合總共三項學術文獻。

用同樣的好奇心了解你的**技術架構**：請 agents 協助你理解每項技術及各部分如何連接，然後用自己的話解釋選擇原因、功能和限制。書目使用 Markdown 整理，無需另用書目管理工具。
